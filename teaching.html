Subject Portfolio

Classes & Curricula Taught

Aligned with College Board Advanced Placement and Washington State Secondary Mathematics & Science standards.

Grades 11–12 AP College Board Approved

AP Physics 1: Algebra-Based

Covers kinematics, dynamics, circular motion, simple harmonic motion, momentum, work, energy, and rotational mechanics through guided laboratory inquiry.

Key Learning Outcomes:

  • Construct free-body vector diagrams to analyze complex mechanical equilibrium.
  • Design and execute independent laboratory experiments using sensors and sensors.
  • Quantitative and qualitative problem solving for AP Physics exam preparation.
Grades 11–12 AP College Board Approved

AP Calculus AB / BC

Focuses on rate of change, differential calculus, definite and indefinite integration, fundamental theorem of calculus, and series expansions.

Key Learning Outcomes:

  • Apply graphical, numerical, analytical, and verbal representations of functions.
  • Solve real-world optimization and accumulation problems in engineering and science.
  • Develop rigorous mathematical proofs and logical justification steps.
Grades 9–11 High School Honors Curriculum

Algebra II & Honors Algebra

Comprehensive study of linear systems, polynomial functions, radical equations, logarithmic scales, and introduction to trigonometry.

Key Learning Outcomes:

  • Master vertex and standard form transformation of quadratic functions.
  • Analyze real-world exponential growth, decay, and compound interest models.
  • Build foundational confidence for advanced STEM coursework.
Grade 12 Interdisciplinary Capstone

STEM Engineering Capstone

Senior design lab where students apply physics, mathematics, and computer modeling to engineer physical prototypes and present peer-reviewed research.

Key Learning Outcomes:

  • Collaborate in engineering project teams using iterative design sprints.
  • Utilize CAD modeling, 3D printing, and microcontroller micro-electronics.
  • Present technical capstone findings to industry STEM panellists.
Instructional Framework

Example Weekly Lesson Structure

A representative weekly structure illustrating how inquiry, conceptual mastery, and assessment are balanced.

Day / Focus Core Classroom Activity Student Assessment & Homework Differentiated Support
Monday
Conceptual Anchor		 Interactive demonstration or real-world phenomenon observation; peer hypothesis discussion. Guided reading notes & conceptual check ticket (15 mins). Visual graphic organizers provided.
Tuesday
Mathematical Modeling		 Deriving equations from physical observations; step-by-step teacher modeling and whiteboard practice. Problem Set 1 (Algebraic & Graphical calculations). Formula scaffold sheets available.
Wednesday
Hands-On Inquiry Lab		 Collaborative laboratory investigation using sensor technology and physical apparatus. Data analysis and vector free-body lab notebook entry. Heterogeneous lab group pairing.
Thursday
Synthesis & AP Prep		 AP-style free response question breakdown in small groups; error analysis of sample responses. Targeted practice quiz with immediate feedback rubric. 1-on-1 office hours assistance.
Friday
Review & Mastery		 Student-led problem presentation on whiteboards; concept synthesis and reflection. Weekly self-assessment & correction submission. Extension challenges for advanced learners.
Teacher FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Insights into classroom philosophy, assessment standards, and support options for parents and students.

I establish a culture of mutual respect, clear boundaries, and collaborative routines from day one. I view behavior as communication, focusing on restorative practices, clear expectations, and positive reinforcement to keep the focus on learning.
I use a Concrete-Representational-Abstract (CRA) instructional framework. Students begin with physical manipulatives or visual geometric simulations, transition to graphic diagrams, and gradually advance to symbolic equations. I also offer daily office hours and peer tutoring.
Technology is used purposefully to enhance conceptual understanding. We use Desmos for dynamic function graphing, PhET Interactive Simulations for virtual physics experiments, and Google Classroom for transparent assignment tracking.
Communication is proactive and consistent. In addition to bi-weekly gradebook updates, I send regular class newsletters highlighting upcoming projects and reach out personally via email or phone whenever a student demonstrates exceptional progress or needs additional intervention.
